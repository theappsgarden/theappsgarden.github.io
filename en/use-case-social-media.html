Communications · sports club

Keep social media up to date without losing half the day

Illustrative examples based on hypothetical organisations. These are not real clients; figures are assumptions, not measured results.

230 h/year · Potential time recovered

The organisation

A sports club with 800 members and a small office organises activities, opens registrations and communicates schedule changes. Nobody has social media as their main role. Coaches and coordinators send details by email and message; an administrator prepares posts when other duties allow.

The challenge

Some activities are announced late, the same information is rewritten for each channel, and last-minute changes require checking scheduled posts. The club does not need constant content production: it needs to communicate what is happening regularly, with less preparation time.

What we would examine

  1. Questionnaire covering channels, audiences, frequency and information contributors.
  2. Review interview following an activity from planning to publication.
  3. A second round to measure preparation time and identify commonly missing details.
  4. Draft tests using club activities, checking quality and scheduling on the chosen channels.

From confirmed information to publication

  1. Shared intake. A form would collect the activity, date, location, registration link and authorised materials. Missing essential details would be requested before proceeding.
  2. Channel-specific drafts. AI would adapt copy to the club’s voice without inventing schedules, results or sponsors.
  3. Template-based design. Branded formats for announcements, results and reminders, using photographs approved by the club.
  4. Approval and scheduling. A person would review a batch of posts; only approved posts would enter the calendar.
  5. Change management. A cancellation or schedule change would trigger an alert to review pending posts.
  6. Follow-up. A monthly summary would show what was published, what attracted interest and what information needs improving.

Automation would coordinate tasks; AI would help write and adapt. Not every step needs AI.

Time-recovery assumptions

  • Gathering information and preparing the calendar: two hours reduced to one per week, 46 hours a year.
  • Writing and adapting posts: three hours reduced to one per week, 92 hours a year.
  • Preparing visuals with templates: two hours reduced to one per week, 46 hours a year.
  • Scheduling and preparing a results summary: 1.5 hours reduced to 0.5 per week, 46 hours a year.

What we would avoid

Publishing without supervision or creating daily content just to fill the calendar. We would not automate replies to complaints, sensitive issues or messages about minors. Even without dedicated social-media staff, someone must be responsible for validating information and approving posts.

Proposed 90-day roadmap

  1. Month 1 · Prepare. Choose useful channels, define templates and organise information intake.
  2. Month 2 · Test. Start with activities and registrations, reviewing every draft.
  3. Month 3 · Connect and measure. Activate scheduling for approved content and change alerts; check actual time savings.

What the figures mean

Moving from 8.5 to 3.5 hours a week would free 230 hours a year over 46 weeks. The remaining 3.5 hours include providing information, reviewing and approving. At €22/hour, that is €5,060 of working capacity before tools, maintenance and implementation. Comments and private messages would be measured separately. The aim is regular communication without displacing the club’s main work; no increase in followers or registrations is assumed.

How we estimate

For each task: current weekly hours minus target weekly hours, multiplied by 46 weeks. The total is (8.5 − 3.5) × 46 = 230 hours a year. These are assumptions, not measured results or guaranteed financial savings. Your Study would validate the workflow, timings and costs; implementation would be a separate engagement.
